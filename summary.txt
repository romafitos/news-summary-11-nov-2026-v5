World news, Wednesday 11 November 2026. US-brokered Russia-Ukraine talks in Abu Dhabi yielded a prisoner swap of 1,000 to 1,500 a side, Kushner said, but no ceasefire and no territory agreement (Reuters). Russian strikes hit six power substations; an Odesa market blast killed three.

At COP31, UN chief Antonio Guterres urged windfall taxes on energy profits, noting the nine largest firms made $95 billion in a quarter against a Fund of $600 million (UN News). He called for a "climate justice compact" and warned of 2.5-plus degrees even if plans hold.

US prosecutors charged three men over alleged Iran-backed plots, one aimed at RAF Fairford, and Treasury chief Bessent was moved to a military base after threats (New York Times). Iran's envoy said Hormuz, shut 250-plus days, "will never return" to its pre-war state (Al Jazeera).

In Spain, four million workers struck over housing, the first in 14 years, ahead of a 29 November election (Reuters). Rent averages 1,176 euros — 98.7 percent of a young worker's monthly pay (Youth Council).

British police arrested a 41-year-old US Army officer at a NATO corps base near Gloucester, accused of spying for Russia; the Kremlin denied any link (Reuters).

Alexander Darchiev, Russia's US ambassador since March 2025, died suddenly in Moscow aged 66 (Reuters). A spokeswoman cited chronic illness.

Kenya discharged its last Ebola patient, starting a 42-day countdown to an Ebola-free declaration on 23 December (Reuters). Nine cases in all: seven recoveries, two deaths.

Israel's new parliament was sworn in with no government formed; Netanyahu's bloc won 53 of 120 seats (AP). Israeli strikes in Gaza killed at least eight, with floods swamping 70,000 tents (Al Jazeera/CARE).

In Sudan's Blue Nile state, RSF drones struck displaced civilians heading to shelters in Damazin, killing nine (Xinhua).

Nvidia's board met late on Wednesday on ousting CEO Jensen Huang over a China spy scandal; shares slid over 8% after hours (Reuters).
